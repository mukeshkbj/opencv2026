# Storylines for the second cut

All three keep every real screen and number. All three add a music bed, sound on visible actions (stamp, shutter, wipe, VALID / NOT VALID), and burned-in captions for judges who watch muted. Target length: 1:45 to 2:00 (the current cut is 2:27).

## A. Scratch or reflection? (recommended: the hook is the product's own hardest case, shown in the first second)

- **Device and form:** a question with the input; match cuts on one red box that travels from shot to shot.
- **What the viewer sees:** the film opens on the real car photo with two red boxes and one line: "One is a scratch. One is a reflection." The rental company bills for both. Twice answers the question on screen, then proves it can't be faked.
- **First two seconds:** frame 0 is the car panel with both boxes already drawn. At 0.8 s the caption lands: "One is a scratch. One is a reflection." A low pulse starts.
- **Beats:** the question (0:00) → the $440 bill slams in, stamp hits (0:05) → "the company has an AI scanner; the renter has a phone" (0:14) → the ghost guide locks, the shutter fires (0:20) → the side-step photo: the reflection slides away, the scratch stays, the question is answered (0:30) → the room, the shadow ruled out (0:48) → the signed report (1:00) → one byte changed: NOT VALID, the music drops out (1:10) → how it runs, in 12 s (1:18) → 88% / 100% / 0 of 22 (1:30) → "Take them twice." (1:45).
- **Look:** the product's paper, Gambetta and signal red. The red box is the one carrier.
- **Sound:** a tense, minimal pulse at 100 to 110 bpm under the question and the bill. It opens up when the ghost locks, cuts to silence on NOT VALID, and lands one hit on the end line.

## B. Minutes after the return

- **Device and form:** a moment in someone's day; one continuous take along a clock.
- **What the viewer sees:** a phone lock screen with a bill notification "minutes after returning a car" (The Drive's words). The camera never cuts: it pulls out of the notification into the receipt, then into Twice on the same phone, and back to the notification, now answered with a signed report.
- **First two seconds:** a phone screen, a notification sliding in: "Damage charge: $440.00".
- **Beats:** the notification → the receipt → "it was there when I picked it up" → move-in photos at pickup → the ghost at return → the report → reply to the bill with the bundle → numbers → end.
- **Look:** a phone frame on the paper ground; the product inside it at full size.
- **Sound:** a notification tone, then a warm, steady bed at 95 bpm that resolves on the reply.
- **Risk:** the phone UI around the product (the notification, the mail reply) would be drawn, not real, so it must be labelled illustrative.

## C. Two photos, one truth (the bold one)

- **Device and form:** type carries the argument; split screen to one. Every scene starts as two panes, move-in and move-out, that slide together and lock into one frame.
- **What the viewer sees:** giant kinetic type ("Same spot." "Same light?" "Same car?") cut on the beat between pairs of real photos that lock together. The product appears as the thing that does the locking.
- **First two seconds:** two halves of the car photo slam together from the left and right on a downbeat; "Same spot." lands across the seam.
- **Beats:** pairs lock on the beat → the bill as a pair (charge vs photo) → the ghost guide → the agent's side-step pair → the report → the verify pair (VALID / NOT VALID) → numbers as pairs → end.
- **Look:** full-bleed photo halves, giant Gambetta, hard cuts.
- **Sound:** a driving 120 bpm track; every lock is a hit.
- **Risk:** it needs a strong music track to work, and it explains less, so the agent's reasoning gets less time.
